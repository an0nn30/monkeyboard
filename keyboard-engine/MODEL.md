# English keyboard model · build 19

The keyboard extension links a static Rust library and calls `mb_rank_v1` for spelling candidates and next-word predictions. Swift supplies current keyboard geometry and allowed left context, consults the native spell checker and supplementary lexicon, and owns text insertion and undo. Rust never receives a document handle or stores typed messages.

Build 19 keeps the transformer checkpoint and focuses on spaces. The Rust policy corroborates exact two-word UIKit suggestions against phrase counts and grammatical context; a supplemental whole-word dictionary protects intentional compounds. [Full-sentence results and tradeoffs](../Benchmarks/build19/README.md).

Build 18 scores “us” against “is” with the same decoder once a supported subject has been followed by an article and another word. A new aggregate table of word and adjacent-pair counts also supports missing spaces, with weaker splits waiting for evidence from the next word. [Fresh whole-sentence benchmark](../Benchmarks/build18/README.md).

Build 17 retains the 15M decoder. Its spelling policy checks aggregate mistake/repair evidence conditioned on the preceding word, alongside intentional phrases from the clean training messages. A separately confirmed “amanor” repair waits for a compatible following noun. Only aggregate counts or mappings are used, with no new collection of typed messages. [Whole-sentence simulator comparison](../Benchmarks/build17/README.md).

Build 16 learns recurring typing errors and next-word counts from the supplied noisy/corrected sentence pairs. Training groups all variants of a normalized corrected sentence together. Only aggregated typo mappings and phrase counts enter the keyboard. [Training and held-out results](../Benchmarks/build16/README.md).

Build 15 adds a second spelling search for unresolved lowercase typos. The Rust ranker can recognize two adjacent-key substitutions, an omission with a transposition, and a missing space supported by local word counts. Swift consults it after the existing spelling policy. A sentence-opening “Lets” can be revised after a verb. The 15M decoder can also compare “were” and “we've” after “what”, once a following participle is available. Next-word ranking mixes the decoder's probabilities with observed one- to four-word phrase counts. The displayed transformer vocabulary grows from 577 to 1,226 words. The base transformer weights stay at their build 14 checkpoint. Regenerate the spelling counts with `python3 keyboard-engine/training/edit_phrases.py`. [Typing and prediction benchmark](../Benchmarks/build15/README.md).

Build 14 retrains the 35,848-byte “leek”/“leak” readout on 40,514 authored training contexts. The same frozen 15M decoder provides the features. The keyboard can now wait for up to five following words for this pair. The readout scores the actual report first. For borderline fixture reports it also scores the location with a neutral speaker. Positive decisions require evidence of a fixture, damage, a repair or a fluid. Food, handling and literal-spelling cues can veto an edit. Common fixture words outside the base vocabulary map to known words for this review, including “bathtub” to “basin” and “hose” to “pipe”. [Build 14 benchmark](../Benchmarks/build14/README.md).

Build 13 adds a 35,848-byte spelling classifier for lowercase “leek” and its sentence-initial capitalization. It considers the literal and “leak” after a word boundary. The original decoder has a token for “leak” and no token for “leek”. Both senses use the same marker. A linear classifier trained on 7,206 programmatically assembled contexts reads the frozen five-layer transformer's internal states. The features include the mean of recent left-context states, the last word embedding and the final sentence state. The embedding projection is precomputed in the shipped weights. The automatic decision also requires a plausible report of a problem, a recognized fixture and supporting context. Food, handling and literal-spelling cues veto a change. This is a targeted classifier for one pair. Rebuild it with `python keyboard-engine/training/neural/spelling_review.py` in the PyTorch training environment. The updated Core ML export exposes the final hidden states as well as the existing next-word logits. [Build 13 benchmark](../Benchmarks/build13/README.md).

Build 12 defaults to the **Transformer · 15M** setting. The compact build 11 path supplies immediate commits and provisional suggestions. The full decoder ranks the reviewed next-word vocabulary and revisits eligible spelling candidates when one to three subsequent words become available. The lookback score combines the candidate’s left-context logit with the normalized log likelihood of the following tokens. A high margin and supporting continuation evidence are required for general misspellings. Dictionary-accepted lowercase `gos` has a separate third-person subject check and inflection prior. Explicitly chosen spellings, protected fields and mid-document edits bypass lookback. Testing uses the same checkpoint and vocabulary in the extension and simulator. See the [lookback replay](../Benchmarks/build12/README.md).

Build 11 keeps the build 10 neural network and adds a compact **semantic reranker**, trained by factorizing a weighted term-by-definition matrix (latent semantic analysis). It covers 16,332 terms in 80 dimensions, including *boot*, which the build 10 neural vocabulary missed. Training uses Princeton [WordNet 3.0](https://wordnet.princeton.edu/) noun definitions, synonyms and related senses plus the pre-existing training vocabulary; no device input or benchmark sentences are used. On an eligible misspelling after a determiner, the Rust engine compares two distinct cues from anywhere in the available current sentence against plausible noun spellings. It asks for a strong match and separation from alternatives before overriding an existing choice. A separate conservative rule can accept the first spelling candidate after an exact double tap or transposition. The sentence-context switch controls both refinements. No new next-word model or private keyboard API.

Training: download [WordNet-3.0.tar.gz](https://wordnetcode.princeton.edu/3.0/WordNet-3.0.tar.gz), then run `python keyboard-engine/training/semantics/build.py /path/to/WordNet-3.0.tar.gz` (Python 3.13, numpy 2.5.4, scipy 1.18.1). The script checks the pinned SHA-256; it never reads evaluation cases. The derived int8 vectors, vocabulary and [license](model/semantics/WordNet-LICENSE.txt) are checked in. License text is also shipped in the app under Prediction data credits. [Build 11 side-by-side results](../Benchmarks/build11/README.md).

Build 10 adds a second C entry point for a quantized causal-attention word scorer trained from scratch. On eligible misspellings it scores the system/Rust candidate pool against the current sentence **before the caret**, up to 128 tokens and 8 KiB of host-exposed text. It uses a learned last-word query to pool earlier keys/values, followed by a hidden layer and candidate-specific output logits. Earlier tokens in the compact model are unordered apart from the last token. We preproject the per-word attention tables offline. No private Apple keyboard API, external pretrained weights, right-of-caret prediction, network, or user learning. Switch off *Use sentence context* in Keyboard Settings to restore build 9's correction policy. Cargo's `--no-default-features` also omits the weights and retains the FFI fallback.

We also trained and kept the spec's first research decoder: 15,185,408 parameters, five causal blocks, six heads, width 384, learned positions, 32-token inference, 8,192 vocabulary entries, next-word cross entropy (ignore unknown targets). The FP16 checkpoint is stored under `research/decoder15m`. Build 12 ships its Core ML export in the keyboard extension and runs it on a serial worker. Reproduce the conversion and output comparison with `training/neural/export_decoder.py` using torch 2.8 and coremltools 9. Both models used 75,433 deduplicated training sentences, 3,162 validation sentences, the pinned public Tatoeba/Taskmaster English sources listed below, and no real user text. The held-out replay sources were excluded by sentence hash or whole dialogue ID; threshold selection used separate corrupted samples. Compact validation NLL 4.723 (epoch 7), decoder 4.381 (epoch 3). We have not run the 30M/50M sweeps, on-device decoder latency/RSS gate, or personalization. The compact scorer is a reversible prototype; its automatic replacement policy trades a small increase in wrong replacements for improved recovery. [Fresh side-by-side tests and limitations](../Benchmarks/build10/README.md).

Reproduce with Python 3.13, torch 2.15.0 and numpy 2.5.4, using the pinned source download below:

```sh
python keyboard-engine/training/neural/train.py /tmp/mb-taskmaster-self-dialogs.json
python keyboard-engine/training/neural/train.py /tmp/mb-taskmaster-self-dialogs.json --transformer --epochs 5 --output /tmp/mb-decoder15m
```

Build 9 adds agreement between system spelling guesses and nearby-key candidates, a bounded search for words with two typing errors, and checks of short fragments against frequent observed phrases. For example, after “to figure”, the training table gives “out” 13 occurrences and the next candidate one. This helps resolve “ut” when the user presses Space. The [typing-session benchmark](../Benchmarks/mistaps/cases.tsv) exercises both samples. The expected spellings there reflect inferred intent.

**Changes from build 7.** The lexical tables are unchanged. The ranker now recognizes canonical first-person forms (including short inputs) and common missing-apostrophe contractions. The Swift policy lets the first-person pronoun take its correct case mid-sentence; acronyms, personal lexicon entries, host field exclusions and literal undo still take precedence. Ambiguous dictionary words such as `ill`, `id`, and `were` are not expansion rules. Confidence is a release-tested heuristic, not a probability.

Next-word suggestions combine the last one to four words within a sentence. Specific phrases get more weight, but single-word-only matches back off to general English when available. Predictions use two reviewed output vocabulary lists; the added texting list does not change the automatic spelling vocabulary. A sorted context table holds count lists as read-only data, rather than allocating a vector for every context. English only. No swipe decoding, user profiling or background training.

Tatoeba's 256-prompt general holdout is unchanged. Taskmaster is split by conversation ID before counting, and normalized utterances shared with the test partition are removed from training. The 256 Taskmaster evaluation contexts come from unseen dialogues and contain only reviewed-vocabulary words. This is easier than unrestricted free text. Exact normalized benchmark sentences are excluded from the prediction counts; ordinary short phrases overlap by design. The authored texting examples are a development benchmark and cannot establish independent real-world accuracy. See [benchmarks](../Benchmarks/build8/README.md) and the [personalization roadmap](PERSONALIZATION.md).
